# Concept: Zettelkasten in Citadel

The **Zettelkasten** (German for "slip-box") is a method for managing complex knowledge through interconnected, atomic notes. Citadel is purpose-built to support this workflow.

## The Core Ingredients
To implement a Zettelkasten in Citadel, you rely on three fundamental behaviors:

### 1. Atomicity
Every scroll should cover **one single idea**. 
- Don't write a 5,000-word essay. Write 20 small scrolls that link to each other.
- Atomic scrolls are easier to link, easier to refactor, and create a denser "knowledge web."

### 2. Connectivity (Bi-Directional Linking)
Connections are more valuable than the contents themselves.
- Use `[[id]]` or the link tool to connect an idea to its predecessors or related concepts.
- Citadel tracks these relationships, showing you **Backlinks** (where the current scroll is mentioned) to provide context.

### 3. The Archives
Information shouldn't be buried in folders. 
- Use **Tags** and **Metadata** to surface scrolls dynamically.
- Instead of clicking through folders, use **The Archives** to query for specific concepts across your entire **Keep**.

## Discovery via the Tag Graph
The Zettelkasten method thrives on **serendipity**—finding connections you didn't know existed.
1. Tag your scrolls with broad concepts.
2. Open the **Tag Graph**.
3. Look for "clusters" where unrelated topics start to overlap.
4. This intersection is often where new insights and original ideas are born.
